How this calculator works

Most heat pump calculators online ask you for your postcode and bedroom count, then give you a single number with no working shown. This one shows the maths so you can sanity-check it against your actual bills.

The calculation is straightforward:

  • Gas boiler annual cost = (heat demand ÷ boiler efficiency) × gas unit price + (gas standing charge × 365)
  • Heat pump annual cost = (heat demand ÷ SCOP) × electricity unit price + any standing charge difference

The two numbers that matter most are boiler efficiency (typically 85-92% for a modern condensing combi) and SCOP (the heat pump's seasonal efficiency). A heat pump with SCOP 3.5 turns one unit of electricity into 3.5 units of heat. Since electricity costs roughly 4× more per kWh than gas, you need a SCOP above ~3.0 just to break even on a standard tariff.

Why your real running cost might differ

This is an estimate. Three things move it up or down significantly:

1. The installation, not the heat pump

A poorly-sized heat pump or undersized radiators can drop your real-world SCOP from 3.5 to 2.5 or lower. The single biggest predictor of running cost is installer quality, not equipment brand. MCS certification is necessary but not sufficient — look for installers who do a proper room-by-room heat loss survey rather than a quick whole-house estimate.

2. Your tariff matters more than you'd think

On a standard variable tariff, a heat pump and a modern gas boiler often cost roughly the same to run. The economics shift dramatically on a heat-pump-specific time-of-use tariff. Cosy Octopus has cheaper rates twice a day, and a heat pump with hot water tank can pre-heat during those windows. Customers report 20-30% lower running costs vs. standard tariffs.

3. House fabric and habits

Heat pumps work best at lower flow temperatures (40-50°C vs. 65-75°C for boilers). That means longer, gentler heating cycles — closer to leaving it on all day at 19°C than blasting it from cold to 22°C in the evening. Homes with poor insulation or radiators that can't handle low flow temps will see worse running costs.

What about upfront cost and the £7,500 grant?

This calculator focuses on running costs only. Upfront cost is a separate question and depends heavily on your property — typical air source heat pump installs are £8,000-14,000 before grant, £500-6,500 after the £7,500 Boiler Upgrade Scheme grant.

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme is open until 2028 or until funds run out, whichever comes first. To qualify your home needs a valid EPC with no outstanding insulation recommendations, and the installer must be MCS-certified.

Frequently asked questions

Is a heat pump cheaper to run than a gas boiler?

On a standard tariff with a well-designed install (SCOP 3.5+), it's usually similar or slightly cheaper. On a heat-pump-specific tariff like Cosy Octopus, £200-500 annual savings are realistic. With a poor install (SCOP under 3.0) on a standard tariff, a heat pump can cost more to run than a modern gas boiler.

What is SCOP and why does it matter?

SCOP (Seasonal Coefficient of Performance) measures how efficiently a heat pump turns electricity into heat across a full year, accounting for cold winter days and milder shoulder seasons. A SCOP of 3.5 means 1 kWh of electricity produces 3.5 kWh of heat. Higher SCOP = lower running costs. Manufacturer-claimed SCOPs are tested in lab conditions and often higher than what you'll achieve in a real home.

Should I remove my gas supply?

If you have no other gas appliances (hob, fire), yes. The gas standing charge is roughly £117/year and adds nothing if you're not using gas. You'll need to arrange disconnection with your gas supplier — a one-off cost of £100-£500 depending on the situation. Keep it if you have a gas hob you don't want to replace.

How accurate is this calculator?

It's an estimate. The biggest uncertainty is your real-world SCOP, which depends on the installer, your house, and how you use the system. For a real number, an MCS-certified installer will perform a heat loss survey as part of their quote — that's the most accurate figure you'll get short of installing the system.

Why are heat pumps more expensive to run on standard electricity tariffs?

UK electricity is currently taxed and levied more heavily than gas — what's known as the "spark gap." Government policy is moving to rebalance this, but for now it means heat pump owners benefit significantly from time-of-use tariffs that exploit cheaper overnight or off-peak rates. A heat pump on a standard tariff is fine; a heat pump on a smart tariff is where the savings appear.
